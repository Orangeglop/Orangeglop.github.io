I’ve always kept a box with me since I was young, not ONE box, but A box - filled with memorabilia like old tickets, bottle caps, handwritten notes, and photographs. These objects might seem trivial to some, but to me, they represent a memory capsule: a collection of personal artifacts imbued with history and sentiment.

![](/assets/research/the-shoebox/the-shoebox-1.webp)

Why?

The items in the box are tied to the moments and people in my life, sometimes with a very specific experience.
For example: bottle caps of the craft beers I had with some friends in Romania, tea boxes from a trip to Dinant, or a napkin printed with birds from the second Christmas dinner in 2023, I can name them all.

![](/assets/research/the-shoebox/the-shoebox-4.webp)

Imagine they all have a different color shade representing the actants, and each linked with the specific memories drawn in that same color. Perhaps they are all color-less to me if it was the same item outside of the box, but within the box, they are a collection with different colors. Every time I open and go through these items, it feels like I was reliving the memories.

![](/assets/research/the-shoebox/the-shoebox-5.webp)

The act of collecting involves a synthetic process of assembling temporal fragments, creating an illusion of continuity that is inherently tied to a sense of loss. This loss, rather than being merely negative, serves a productive function in defining the temporal nature of collections. And when I cannot recall anything about a specific item anymore, the item then will be taken out of the box. The consequence comes after is that there will be space for newer items to be placed in.

![](/assets/research/the-shoebox/the-shoebox-6.webp)

Must there be others with the same "fetish" as me?
I asked some of my friends about their own experience with this same type of practice, most of them – mainly are female – have at least one.
