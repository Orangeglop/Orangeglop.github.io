Ho Chi Minh city is one of the most vibrant cities in Vietnam. This image is portraited by the high-rise buildings and busy business streets. But aside the modern stores and restaurants, another side of this city is built up from the majority of population here - the informal economy. These informal economies provide different services: F&B - from daily filling meals to small bites of snacks, salon, miscellaneous items - household items, toys, etc.

And small plastic stools - the type you need to squat down with your knees sit higher than your hip while sitting, are the most common items from these services on the street you could see.

![](/assets/research/the-stool/The stool - 1.webp)

The plastic stools are convenient, somewhat cheap, light, and unconsciously became a shared icon for all informal economies in the city. They can be both the chair and table if you are up for a meal on the side of the street - which are affordable for most hard-labor working people, students, and gig-workers. They can be a temporary shelf for the owners’ tools - spoons, stirring straws, scissors, etc. They can sometimes become fighting weapons when there are “minor” conflicts between vendors.

![](/assets/research/the-stool/The stool - 2.webp)

But behind this iconic mundane item lays more complicated interaction between the formal and informal activities in the city, how the government control them, and how the non-government taking advantage of them.
